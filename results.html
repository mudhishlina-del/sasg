00 · Three methods, four KPIs

What does combining Fourier with peak shaving change?

Fourier only models the daily cycle but moves no load. Peak shaving only shifts load using the measured hours. The proposed method plans which hours to shift, and by how much, from the five-harmonic curve, then applies the shift to the measured load. All shifting methods use the same 2,124.45 MWh budget and 464.06 MW hourly limit.

What changed? Adding the Fourier planning layer leaves the clean-data peak cut unchanged (3.29% for both) but makes it more robust to noisy input: 3.17% vs 2.84% mean and 2.43% vs 1.65% in the worst 5% of trials at 3% synthetic noise. Moving averages give similar robustness, so the benefit is smoothing; Fourier adds an interpretable amplitude–phase model. The cost is a steeper maximum ramp, 686 vs 489 MW/h.

A tie on peak reduction, a trade-off on ramp

Peak reduction measures how far the highest hour falls. Maximum ramp is the largest change between consecutive hours, so lower is smoother.

04 · Synthetic noise and benchmarks

Does guidance from a smoothed curve help when the input is noisy?

Each trial adds the same synthetic Gaussian noise to the one Riyadh profile before every method plans its schedule. The schedules are then scored on the original profile. There are 200 paired trials per level. Without noise the methods tie on peak reduction.

Moving-average curves (3 h and 5 h) are smoothing benchmarks. Their results are close to Fourier K5, so the advantage over peak shaving only comes from smoothing in general and is not specific to Fourier. These are results under simulated noise, not field validation.

Reporting reference

The view follows the hourly MW-by-hour structure used for demand-response reporting in the Saudi Arabian Grid Code, May 2026, Schedule 7.[5] The Grid Code lists demand-side response as a system service (§2.14), describes hourly reporting at individual connection points (§4.5) and specifies quarter-hourly integration for peak-demand metering (§7.7.2).

This simulation uses hourly estimates of total regional load, so the Code serves only as a reporting reference here. It is not a compliance assessment or operational validation.
